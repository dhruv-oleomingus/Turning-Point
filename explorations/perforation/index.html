Turning Point

The columns of Turning Point are cut with patterns grown by a reaction–diffusion process — two simulated substances, one feeding and one consuming the other, settling into tendrils, labyrinths and spots.

The plate is cut into squares, as the columns are, and the squares are grouped into blocks — single squares, pairs, bars, corners. Each block holds the pattern at its own grain, dense, medium or open, cut through the plate or drawn only as an outline.

Tap to start a pattern; drag from it to stretch it — it grows where you pull it. Pull it across the edge of a square and that square takes on the pattern you brought: it joins the block you came from, with its grain and its cut.

Press play: a line crosses the plate from left to right. Every cut shape it passes through — a tendril, a cell — sounds once, in the voice of the rows it sits in. The rows play in pairs, from the top: pluck, keys, bass, and round again — marked beside the plate — each shape higher the higher it sits within its pair. Outlined squares stay silent. The more shapes still ahead, the slower it goes. The pattern can be grown, and pulled about, while it plays.
